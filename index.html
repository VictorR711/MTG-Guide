Magic: The Gathering

A beginner's guide to the world's oldest trading card game (1993). Learn the rules, the colors and how to build your first deck.

Basics

The four concepts that make everything else make sense.

You are a Planeswalker

Every player is a wizard who travels between worlds. Your deck is your arsenal: spells, creatures and lands. You start with 20 life (40 in Commander) and whoever brings the opponent to 0 wins.

The deck is your strategy

Before playing, you build your own deck: 60 cards minimum (100 in Commander). You can only include up to 4 copies of the same card, and basic lands have no limit.

Mana is energy

It's produced by lands. You play one per turn and tap it to pay costs. Each basic land gives one color: Plains (white), Island (blue), Swamp (black), Mountain (red) and Forest (green).

Zones of the game

Library (your deck), hand, battlefield (what's in play), graveyard (used cards) and exile (cards removed from the game).

Anatomy of a card

  1. Name: top left.
  2. Mana cost: top right. The symbols show the color; a number is mana of any type.
  3. Illustration: the art in the center.
  4. Type line: says whether it's a creature, sorcery, etc., and its subtype (for example, "Creature — Angel").
  5. Text box: the abilities. Below that, the italic text is just flavor and doesn't affect the game.
  6. Power/Toughness: bottom right, creatures only. Power is the damage it deals; toughness is how much damage it can take before dying.

Golden rules

Rule 1: If a card contradicts the rules, the card wins. The card's text takes priority over the general rules.
Rule 2: One land per turn. You can only play one, unless a card says otherwise.
Rule 3: Pay the full cost. You can't cast a spell if you can't pay its entire cost.
Rule 4: A creature can't attack the turn it arrives. It has "summoning sickness," unless it has Haste.
Rule 5: No cards, you lose. If you have to draw from an empty deck, you lose the game.
Rule 6: Anything that says "may" is optional. Anything that says "must" is not.

The five colors

Each color has its own philosophy. Almost every deck uses one or two.

    Your turn, phase by phase

    Phase 1 Beginning You untap your cards, resolve upkeep effects and draw a card.
    Phase 2 Main You play a land and cast creatures, sorceries, artifacts and enchantments.
    Phase 3 Combat You declare attackers; the opponent declares blockers; damage is dealt.
    Phase 4 Second main Another chance to cast spells or play your land if you didn't earlier.
    Phase 5 End "Until end of turn" effects wear off. If you have more than 7 cards, you discard down to 7.

    The stack: how spells resolve

    When you cast a spell, it doesn't happen instantly: it goes onto the stack. The opponent can respond with an instant, and that response resolves first. The last card in is the first one to resolve.

    Sorceries can only be played during your main phase. Instants can be played at any time, which is why they let you pull off surprises.

    Formats

    Format Deck Life Best for
    Commander 100 cards, 1 commander, no duplicates 40 The most social. Games of 3 to 4 players.
    Standard 60 cards, recent sets only 20 Competitive play and MTG Arena.
    Modern 60 cards, sets from 2003 onward 20 Competitive play with more variety.
    Draft / Sealed 40 cards, from opened packs 20 Everyone starts on equal footing.
